# USER INTERVIEWS

## Interview 1 — R.K., Backend Developer, Early-Stage Startup (4 people)

**Context:** R.K. is a backend developer at a small bootstrapped startup. They use GitHub Copilot and ChatGPT Plus. I reached out over Twitter DMs after seeing him complain about SaaS costs.

**Key Quotes:**

> "I don't even know what plan we're on for Copilot honestly. My co-founder set it up months ago and we just keep paying."

> "We tried Cursor for a week but went back to Copilot because of habit. Nobody actually compared the pricing."

> "If someone showed me a page that said 'you're wasting $200 a month' with proof, I'd fix it that day. It's not laziness — I just don't have time to research pricing for five different tools."

**Most surprising thing:** He admitted that nobody at his 4-person startup has ever actually reviewed their AI tool subscriptions. They just set them up once and forgot about them. The "set and forget" mentality is exactly the problem SpendLens solves.

**What it changed:** This confirmed that the audit needs to be instant and dead simple. No signups, no onboarding — just pick your tools, see the waste. If it takes more than 2 minutes, people like R.K. won't finish it.

## Interview 2 — S.M., Engineering Manager, Series A Startup (18 people)

**Context:** S.M. manages a team of 12 developers at a Series A fintech startup. They use Cursor Business, ChatGPT Team, and Claude Team. I connected with her through a college alumni network.

**Key Quotes:**

> "We're on Cursor Business because someone said we needed SSO. We have 12 people. I don't think we actually need SSO yet."

> "I had no idea Claude and ChatGPT were basically the same price. We're paying for both because different engineers prefer different ones. That's probably dumb."

> "The thing that would actually make me act is seeing the annual number. $200 a month sounds fine. $2,400 a year makes me go wait, what?"

**Most surprising thing:** She didn't realize they were double-paying for Claude Team AND ChatGPT Team because different engineers had different preferences. When I pointed out the overlap, she immediately said "yeah, we should just pick one." This validated the redundancy detection feature in our audit engine.

**What it changed:** I made the annual savings number the biggest visual element on the results page. Monthly numbers feel small, but the annual total creates urgency. Also reinforced that the consolidation logic (detecting multiple chat LLMs) is one of the most valuable parts of the audit.

## Interview 3 — A.P., Freelance Full-Stack Developer

**Context:** A.P. is a freelance developer who works with 3-4 clients at a time. He pays for Cursor Pro and ChatGPT Plus out of pocket. I asked him about his setup in a Discord server for freelance devs.

**Key Quotes:**

> "I pay $40 a month on AI tools. It's not huge, but it's also not nothing when you're freelance."

> "I've never once checked if there's a cheaper way to get the same thing. I just assume the prices are what they are."

> "Honestly if a tool told me I was already spending well, that would be valuable too. It's like a health checkup — even if you're fine, it's nice to know."

**Most surprising thing:** His comment about "even if you're fine, it's nice to know" was unexpected. I was so focused on finding savings that I almost forgot about users who are already optimized. They still want validation.

**What it changed:** I added honest messaging for low-savings audits. Instead of manufacturing fake savings, the tool now says "You're spending well" and offers a "notify me when new optimizations apply" signup. This keeps the tool trustworthy and still captures the lead.
